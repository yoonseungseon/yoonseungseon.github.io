Working Papers

The Impact of Export Demand Shocks on Domestic Used Car Markets: Evidence from South Korea
with Jay Pil Choi, Seung-Hyun Hong, and Seung Hoon Lee
Abstract

How does foreign demand affect the domestic market for a used durable good? We study the rise in Korea’s used-car exports following Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. Using national registries covering all domestic used-car transfers and export deregistrations, we instrument exports with exposure to Russian demand. We find that dealers purchase more cars from owners at higher prices but sell fewer domestically at higher prices. Private trades increase and their prices fall, suggesting an expansion of private supply. This pattern is puzzling because owners should prefer selling to dealers when dealers pay more. We explain it with a model in which export demand draws more owners to dealers, and dealers with limited capacity buy only the better cars, leaving other cars to be sold privately. Our findings show that a shock to dealers affects all domestic channels, including trades in which dealers do not participate.

How Intermediated Markets Adjust to Demand Shocks: Evidence from Used Cars
with Jay Pil Choi, Seung-Hyun Hong, and Seung Hoon Lee
Abstract

How does a secondary market for a durable good absorb a demand shock when most trade passes through intermediaries, and do intermediaries passively transmit the shock or actively reshape it? We answer both questions using Korean used-car transfers and deregistrations, exploiting the 2019 “No Japan” boycott, a sudden preference shock that left the cars themselves unchanged. The market for Japanese used cars adjusted on three margins: participation, intermediation, and exit. Owners held rather than sold, and volume fell with prices in every channel. Dealer purchase prices fell more than resale prices, widening the spread and shifting price incidence more heavily onto sellers. Older cars exited through export rather than scrappage. The widening does not track inventory holding costs: it is larger where dealers have close substitutes or an export outlet and smaller where sellers can trade privately, consistent with bargaining rents arising from differences in outside options. Our findings suggest that intermediaries reshape shock transmission when the shock affects their two sides unequally, competition is weak, and they can reach buyers that individual sellers cannot.

Air Pollution and Health Burdens across Borders: Evidence from South Korea after China's Plastic Waste Import Ban
with Jaerim Choi
Abstract

This paper examines how China’s 2018 plastic waste import ban shifted environmental and health burdens to South Korea by reshaping global plastic waste trade. Following the ban, South Korea experienced a sharp but geographically uneven surge in plastic waste imports. Using this regional variation, we combine detailed data on trade, air pollution, healthcare utilization, and recycling facilities from 2015 to 2019. We find that regions more exposed to the import shock experienced higher concentrations of PM2.5, consistent with intensified recycling activity, and more emergency-room visits for pollution-sensitive diseases, particularly rhinitis. Our findings highlight a significant transboundary externality of the global plastic waste trade.
